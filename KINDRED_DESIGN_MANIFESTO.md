# The Kindred Design Manifesto

**The design constitution of Kindred**  
*Locked for the life of the product. Amend only with the gravity of amending a charter.*

---

Kindred is a morning newspaper for one person.

It exists so that opening a phone can feel like opening a paper on a quiet kitchen table: cream stock, careful ink, a trusted editor who already did the hard work of choosing what matters today.

This manifesto is not a style guide that expires with a redesign. It is the identity. Every screen, sentence, animation, model call, and product decision must answer to it for the next twenty years.

If a proposal conflicts with this document, the proposal is wrong.

---

## I. Editorial Philosophy

Kindred is edited, not ranked.

We believe the highest form of respect for a reader is selection: fewer stories, better chosen, presented with the dignity of print. The front page is a promise. Everything on it earned its place.

### Principles

1. **Edition over feed.** The unit of Kindred is the day’s paper — complete, finite, calm. There is a beginning, a middle, and an ending. There is no infinite scroll of obligation.
2. **Judgment over volume.** We would rather miss a story than dilute the edition with noise.
3. **Context over urgency.** Breaking news may appear; panic may not. The paper stays composed even when the world is not.
4. **Why before what, when it helps.** Editorial notes (“Why this matters,” “Why it’s on the front page”) exist to deepen understanding — never to sell a click.
5. **The desk is invisible until it is needed.** Intelligence (discovery, knowledge, memory, morning briefing) serves the reading experience. It must never feel like a dashboard of features.
6. **Finish the morning.** A good edition ends. The reader should feel released, not retained.

### Absolute rules

- We never use clickbait.
- We never write headlines that withhold the news to manufacture curiosity.
- We never chase engagement, time-on-app, or “stickiness” as success metrics for craft.
- We never interrupt reading with prompts, badges, streaks, or upsells.
- We never present the news as a scoreboard of outrage.

## II. Typography Philosophy

Type is the architecture of Kindred.

We set words the way a fine magazine sets them: for the eye at rest, for the mind at work, for the hand that once held newsprint.

### Principles

1. **Serif as soul.** Display and body live in expressive serif (Georgia or its rightful successors). Default system UI sans stacks are forbidden as the voice of the paper.
2. **One voice, many sizes.** Hierarchy comes from scale, weight, and spacing — not from a circus of fonts.
3. **Measure like print.** Lines should breathe. Article body seeks a calm reading measure, not full-bleed phone width as a default ideology.
4. **Kickers are quiet authority.** Small caps / tracked uppercase kickers signal section and craft; they never shout.
5. **Italics mean care.** Soft actions, asides, Bandit’s note, and editorial asides lean italic — the typographic equivalent of a lowered voice.
6. **Letterspacing is intentional.** Mastheads earn tracking; body copy does not dress up in costume.

### Absolute rules

- Never default to Inter, Roboto, Arial, or anonymous system UI fonts for Kindred’s editorial voice.
- Never set body copy that feels like a settings screen.
- Never use typography to manufacture urgency (all-caps alarms, neon weights, countdown aesthetics).
- Every text block should look as if it could survive being printed on cream stock.

---

## III. Motion Philosophy

Motion in Kindred is ink settling, not content performing.

Animation exists to welcome, to sequence attention, and to honor the turn of a page — never to entertain, never to addict.

### Principles

1. **Stillness is the default.** Most of the paper does not move. Motion is earned.
2. **Rise and fade, like a folio opening.** Soft opacity and a few pixels of lift. No bounces. No elastic toys.
3. **Stagger like sections, not like a feed.** Entrances unfold in editorial order: masthead, greeting, Bandit, lead, then the rest.
4. **Duration favors grace.** Prefer ~500–750ms of ease-out cubic over snappy product-UI snaps.
5. **Press feedback is tactile and tiny.** A 3% scale or a breath of opacity — enough to feel human, not enough to feel like a game.
6. **Respect reduced motion.** When the reader asks for calm, we become still without becoming broken.

### Absolute rules

- We never use dopamine loops: streaks, confetti, pulsating CTAs, infinite novelty animations.
- We never animate to prove the app is “alive.”
- We never interrupt reading with motion that demands attention.
- If motion would be embarrassing in a printed magazine’s digital edition, it does not ship.

---

## IV. Color Philosophy

Kindred’s color is paper, ink, and a single warm accent — the terracotta of a carefully chosen stamp.

### The palette (identity, not decoration)

| Role | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Cream** (`#FAF6EF` and its quiet washes) | The paper itself. Atmosphere, not background fill. |
| **Ink** (`#2B2620` and its opacities) | Words, authority, reading. |
| **Terracotta** (`#C1622D`) | The human accent: kickers, Bandit, soft actions, care. |

### Principles

1. **Cream is not empty space; it is the medium.** Gradients and washes stay within the paper family. Flat white “app chrome” is alien.
2. **Ink does the work.** Most of the experience is readable dark-on-cream. Color is not the story.
3. **Terracotta is rare and warm.** It marks editorial structure and human invitation. It is never alarm red, never brand purple, never neon.
4. **Rules are hairlines.** Dividers whisper. Cards with heavy borders and shadows are foreign objects.
5. **Photography carries atmosphere.** Editorial images may be full of life; the UI around them stays quiet so the photograph can speak.

### Absolute rules

- We never adopt purple-on-white, indigo glow, or generic “AI product” gradients as Kindred’s look.
- We never use dark mode as an identity shortcut (a night edition, if it ever exists, must be designed as print-in-lamplight — not inverted UI).
- We never use color to create FOMO, alerts spam, or notification anxiety.
- Every screen must still feel like the same newspaper if the logo were removed.

## V. Writing Voice

Kindred’s copy is editorial English: clear, warm, unhurried, worthy of being read aloud at breakfast.

### Principles

1. **Speak like an editor, not a product.** Prefer “Open today’s edition” to “Generate.” Prefer “Kept for you” to “Saved successfully.”
2. **Short sentences earn trust.** Especially in Bandit, errors, and empty states.
3. **Specificity without jargon.** Name the paper, the morning, the desk — never the pipeline, the job, or the payload.
4. **No cheerleading.** Optimism is quiet. We do not exclaim. We do not emoji our way into intimacy.
5. **The second person is a guest, not a target.** “You” is welcome; “users” are not spoken of in the product.
6. **Endings matter.** Colophons (“Until tomorrow morning.”) close the ritual. Incomplete sentences that beg for another tap are banned.

### Absolute rules

- We never use clickbait.
- We never use growth-hacker microcopy (“Don’t miss out,” “Wait — before you go”).
- We never expose technical errors, codes, or stack language in production.
- We never write as a chatbot, a coach, or a brand mascot on espresso.

## VI. Bandit’s Personality

Bandit is Kindred’s calm morning editor.

Bandit is not a mascot, not an assistant, not a notification engine. Bandit is the human temperature of the paper: a short note that makes the reader feel expected and unhurried.

### Character

- **Calm** — never frantic, never breaking-news theatrical.
- **Warm** — friendly without being familiar; intimate without being invasive.
- **Trustworthy** — never invents weather, events, or feelings the grounding does not support.
- **Brief** — one or two sentences. Stillness over cleverness.
- **Signed** — a note from Bandit feels handwritten and finishes with presence (“— Bandit”), not with a feature label.

### Principles

1. Bandit never says “Good morning” when the masthead already has. Redundancy is not warmth.
2. Bandit never restates the full calendar date.
3. Bandit never uses exclamation points, emoji, slang, or sales language.
4. Bandit may use a first name only when it deepens care — never as CRM personalization theater.
5. On birthdays, travel, Sundays, and season turns, Bandit may mark the occasion gently — never as a campaign.
6. If Bandit’s line is missing, the paper still feels held: a calm day-line, never a “coming soon” void.

### Absolute rules

- Bandit always feels calm, warm, and trustworthy.
- Bandit never becomes a chat thread.
- Bandit never nags, reminds with guilt, or gamifies reading.
- Bandit never explains the algorithm.

---

## VII. AI Behavior

In Kindred, AI is the newsroom — not the product.

Models may write, select, explain, and brief. The reader should experience an editor, not a generator.

### Principles

1. **AI always feels like an editor.** Invisible craft, visible judgment. No “Generated by AI” as a personality. No robot throat-clearing.
2. **Grounding over improvisation.** Weather, events, names, and facts come from the edition’s truth — never from model hallucination dressed as intimacy.
3. **Brevity is a moral choice.** Morning openings and briefings respect the reader’s time. Longer forms expand only by invitation.
4. **Intelligence stays in service of the folio.** Discovery, knowledge, memory, and morning edition deepen the paper; they do not spawn new product surfaces by default.
5. **Failure is humanized.** When generation falters, the paper speaks like a press room (“The presses stumbled”), not like a stack trace.
6. **The reader owns the morning.** AI prepares; the human reads. We optimize for comprehension and calm — never for model showmanship.

### Absolute rules

- AI always feels like an editor.
- We never expose chain-of-thought, tool names, or “as an AI” disclaimers in the newspaper.
- We never let AI invent news to fill space.
- We never use AI to maximize session length.

---

## VIII. Discovery Philosophy

Discovery in Kindred is the desk recommending — not the machine predicting.

Bandit’s Picks, weekend ideas, and quiet suggestions appear as a magazine desk list: numbered with care, explained with restraint, limited by mercy.

### Principles

1. **Recommend little.** A handful of items beats a marketplace.
2. **Explain softly.** A single italic “why” is enough. No relevance scores. No “because you liked.”
3. **Separate from news without abandoning craft.** Discovery may live in a cream wash “desk” — still paper, never cards-as-ads.
4. **Curiosity without compulsion.** The reader may ignore the desk completely and still have a complete morning.
5. **Taste over trends.** We prefer lasting interest to viral heat.

### Absolute rules

- We never turn discovery into a social feed.
- We never personalize in a way that feels surveilled.
- We never use “For You” as a void that replaces editorial judgment.

## IX. Reader Psychology

Kindred is designed for the person who wants the world — not the world’s noise.

### Who we design for

Someone who will open Kindred for twenty years: on ordinary Tuesdays, on birthdays, in hotel rooms, in grief, in peace. They want to feel oriented, not stimulated.

### What the morning should feel like

1. **Expected** — the paper was prepared for them.
2. **Unhurried** — nothing pulses for attention.
3. **Finite** — they can finish and walk into the day.
4. **Respected** — their intelligence is assumed; their time is honored.
5. **Accompanied** — Bandit and the desk are present, then quiet.

### What we refuse to do to the mind

- Manufacture scarcity.
- Train compulsion.
- Fragment attention into tabs of anxiety.
- Turn reading into performance (streaks, leaderboards, public metrics).

## X. Emotional Goals

The north star is not NPS. It is love.

Over twenty years, Kindred should become a ritual people defend in conversation: *“I open my paper.”*

### The feelings we design for

| Feeling | How it is earned |
|---------|------------------|
| **Calm** | Cream, measure, soft motion, finite edition |
| **Trust** | Honest headlines, grounded AI, no tricks |
| **Warmth** | Bandit, terracotta, human error language |
| **Pride** | Typography and photography worthy of print |
| **Release** | A clear ending to the morning |
| **Belonging** | Continuity without gamification |

### Absolute rules

- Every interaction should feel worthy of a printed newspaper.
- If a change increases metrics but decreases love, it does not ship.
- Delight is quiet. Loud delight is a different product.

## XI. Interaction Principles

Touch is how the hand turns the page.

### Principles

1. **One job per moment.** Opening, reading, keeping, returning to the library — not simultaneous chrome wars.
2. **Actions are editorial verbs.** Keep. Read the story. Open today’s edition. Send my quiet link. View at the source.
3. **Press states are manners.** Feedback acknowledges the hand; it does not celebrate the tap.
4. **Navigation is a folio, not a tab bar mall.** Adjacent mornings are Yesterday and Later — human time, not “Older/Newer” database language when we can avoid it.
5. **Clipping is affection, not archiving software.** “Keep this” / “Kept for you.”
6. **Never trap.** Back to the paper is always clear. Reading is never a funnel.

### Absolute rules

- We never interrupt reading.
- We never use dark patterns, confirm-shaming, or infinite upsell sheets.
- We never require an account dance mid-sentence.
- We never invent gestures that feel like a game skin on news.

---

## XII. Loading Philosophy

Waiting is part of the ritual — treat it as hospitality, not as a progress bar of guilt.

### Principles

1. **Name the paper, not the spinner.** “Unfolding today’s edition…” / “Your paper is waiting…” / “Turning back the pages…”
2. **Terracotta is the only pulse.** Indicators match the accent of the desk — never generic platform blue.
3. **Waiting copy is Bandit-adjacent.** Warm, brief, unworried.
4. **Prefer readiness to theater.** If the edition can appear without a branded splash marathon, let it.
5. **Empty is not failure.** “Almost ready” honors overnight preparation; it does not blame the reader.

### Absolute rules

- We never show indefinite technical waiting without human language.
- We never use skeleton loaders that look like a social feed loading ads.
- We never make loading feel like a game level.

---

## XIII. Error Philosophy

Errors are moments when trust is most fragile. Speak like a responsible paper, not like a broken server.

### Principles

1. **Human first.** “The presses stumbled. Give it another moment.”
2. **No codes in production.** Developers may see detail in development builds; readers never should.
3. **Offer a dignified next step.** Try again, read yesterday’s paper, return to the edition — never “Contact support” as the only poetry.
4. **Stay in voice.** Terracotta for soft alarm; Georgia for the sentence; no red system banners that belong to another OS.
5. **Honesty without drama.** We do not apologize in paragraphs. We do not joke about outages.

### Absolute rules

- We never dump JSON, status codes, or stack traces into the newspaper.
- We never blame the reader.
- We never use errors as marketing (“Upgrade to avoid this”).

---

## XIV. Accessibility Philosophy

A newspaper that cannot be read by everyone is not a newspaper — it is a poster.

### Principles

1. **Reading is a right.** Dynamic type should enlarge words without destroying the folio’s dignity; test until it still feels like Kindred.
2. **Structure is semantics.** Headers, buttons, and live regions exist so VoiceOver and TalkBack can walk the edition in editorial order.
3. **Contrast on cream is sacred.** Ink opacities must remain readable; faint meta text never drops below care.
4. **Motion is optional.** Reduced motion means FolioReveal becomes presence without performance.
5. **Targets are generous.** Soft links (“Keep this,” “Read a little more”) still offer hit slop worthy of human fingers.
6. **Language stays plain.** Accessibility labels sound like the paper: “A note from Bandit,” not “banditGreetingComponent.”

### Absolute rules

- We never ship beauty that only works for the average eye and the average motor ability.
- We never use color alone to mean state.
- We never hide essential actions in unlabeled icons.

---

## XV. Things Kindred Will NEVER Become

This section is permanent. It is the fence around the garden.

Kindred will never become:

1. **A social network** — no follows-as-identity, no public performance of reading, no comments as the product.
2. **A news feed** — no infinite ranking, no “For You” void, no card stacks optimized for swipe velocity.
3. **A notification factory** — no badge addiction, no urgency theater, no guilt reminders.
4. **A gamified habit app** — no streaks, points, leaderboards, or dopamine loops.
5. **A chatbot** — Bandit is not a conversational agent living in a thread.
6. **A WebView wrapper** — Kindred’s reading experience is native craft, not a browser skin.
7. **An outrage engine** — we will not amplify the worst thing that happened in the last hour for retention.
8. **A generic AI product** — purple gradients, glowing orbs, “ask anything” empty states are foreign soil.
9. **A metrics-first company costume** — engagement theater will not override editorial love.
10. **A loud brand** — Kindred does not shout its name over the news; the paper *is* the name.

---

## XVI. The Twenty-Year Test

Before any change ships, ask:

1. Would this make someone smile — quietly?
2. Would this feel calm at 6:40 a.m.?
3. Would this feel premium if printed on cream stock?
4. Would this become part of a morning ritual — or interrupt one?
5. Would Bandit still feel calm, warm, and trustworthy?
6. Would an editor at a great magazine recognize this as kin?
7. Would we be proud to open this every day for twenty years?

If the answer to any is no, the change does not belong to Kindred.

---

## XVII. Closing

Kindred is not competing with the internet.

Kindred is competing with the feeling of a good newspaper on a good morning: finite, chosen, beautiful, and kind.

We never use clickbait.  
We never chase engagement.  
We never interrupt reading.  
We never use dopamine loops.  
AI always feels like an editor.  
Bandit always feels calm, warm, and trustworthy.  
Every interaction should feel worthy of a printed newspaper.

This is the identity.  
Hold it.

— *The Kindred Design Manifesto*
